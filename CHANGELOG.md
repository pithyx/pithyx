- Backup hints in the manifest (D391, #308): `backend.backup` with `exclude` (named volumes or folders inside them that a backup can leave out, such as caches) and `stop` (stop the backend while the snapshot is taken). The box and `pithyx validate` check them, including that each excluded volume is declared in the Compose file; they are reserved for the planned app backup and not used yet.
- Notification categories (D390, #308): an app names the kinds of notifications it sends in the manifest's `notifications.categories` and each notification's kind with `category` in `notify`. Every user switches single kinds off under **App notifications** in the Notification channels dialog; a muted notification arrives as read, without a toast, email or webhook.
- Store information in the manifest (D389, #308): optional `privacy` (what the app sends out of the box; catalogs require it), `security`, `maturity`, `categories`, `keywords`, `locales`, `screenshots` (files under `frontend/`, at most 8 of 1 MiB), `releaseNotes`, `requirements` (`memory` and `disk` in MB) and `audience`. The install dialog and the app pages show privacy, open source or not, architectures, languages, the security contact and categories; the dialog warns when the box has less memory or the app pool less space than the app needs and installs anyway; updates show their release notes; the catalog tabs filter by category and find apps by their keywords; beta and experimental apps carry a badge. `pithyx validate` checks the screenshots and recommends a security contact for apps with high or critical rights (`cli.manifest.security_missing`).
- Risk tiers for app rights (D388, #308): every special right is device access (`/dev/net/tun`, `/dev/dri`, `/dev/serial`: a hint and a checkbox), high risk (the box's network or processes, capabilities, the filtered `docker.socket`, other devices: a risk text, a checkbox and the password) or root access (`docker.socket.raw`: a red step of its own with the consequences, the app's name typed in and the password; pasting it works, with a reminder that this is not a joke). An app with root access carries the badge **Root access**, is listed under Settings > Security with **Revoke root access**, notifies every other administrator when it is granted and never updates by itself, whatever the update policy says; its update installs with one click. The tiers are data in `compose.v1.rules.json` that the box and `pithyx validate` share.
- `docker.socket.raw` from catalogs (D388, #308): every catalog, official, community or custom, may ship an app with the unfiltered Docker socket, such as a container manager; `app.elevated.sideload_only` is gone. `pithyx validate` warns about it and suggests `docker.socket` (`cli.rights.raw_socket`).
- Elevated rights for app backends (D311, #192): an administrator grants an app the box's network (`host.network`), its processes (`host.pid`), a device node (`device:/dev/...`) or, for root in its container, a Linux capability (`capability:<NAME>`), after confirming with the password and the authenticator code. The confirmation holds for five minutes in that session (`POST /api/auth/step-up`, `GET /api/auth/step-up`). The install dialog, the catalog page and the app's page show each right with its risk; **Grant these rights** can be turned off to install without them; each right can be revoked and granted again on the app's page (`PUT /api/apps/{id}/elevated`), and the backend restarts without a revoked one; an update with a new right waits for an administrator; apps holding one carry the badge **Special rights**. Every step-up and every change goes to the new audit log, shown in the Log Center as **Audit log**. Services with the host's network, processes or a device run outside the user namespace with the app's own user and the device's group, so they cannot run as root. Blocked capabilities and device nodes, `docker.socket` and `host.full` stay refused. Migration 0037; new error codes `auth.step_up.required`, `app.elevated.root_conflict`, `app.elevated.root_required` and `app.device.unavailable`; `pithyx validate` checks the same rules.
- Device classes and the Docker socket for app backends (D387, #224): an app asks for `device:/dev/dri` (every graphics card), `device:/dev/serial` (every USB serial adapter) or `device:/dev/net/tun`, and the box gives its backend the nodes plugged in at each start with their groups. Devices are required unless the manifest lists them in the new `backend.optionalDevices`: a missing required device refuses the install (`app.device.missing`), keeps the backend from starting and stops it when the device goes (the app is `unavailable` with the new reason `device`, its log says why and administrators get a notification), and the app starts again once the device is back; a missing optional one is only a hint. `docker.socket` gives the backend a filtered Docker socket: it sees and controls only the containers and volumes it started, which join the app's network with the backend's limits, never with privileged mode, host namespaces, devices, capabilities, ports or folders of the box; pulls need the permission Internet, building, pushing, loading, committing and pruning are refused, and its containers stop with the backend and go with the app. `docker.socket.raw` is the unfiltered socket, root on the box, only for apps installed from a file (`app.elevated.sideload_only` for catalogs). Both need the step-up of D311 and show their risk in the Store; the app details and the install preview list each device with `optional` and `present`. `pithyx validate` and catalog checks apply the same rules.
- Pool encryption (D384, #190): **Encrypt with a key** in the pool wizard encrypts everything Pithyx keeps on a new pool with ZFS native encryption. The box generates the key (`PXPK-` and 56 characters), shows it once to copy, download or print, and goes on only after you confirm you stored it. The pool unlocks at every start from the key vault on the system disk (preselected; protects against stolen disks, not a stolen box) or stays locked until an administrator types the key (**Unlock…** on the pool card). A locked pool's shared folders, home folders and apps are unavailable until then. Pool cards show **Encrypted** or **Locked** and offer **Change key…** and **Remove key from vault…**. The vault is never part of a configuration backup. New endpoints `POST /api/storage/encryption/keys`, `POST /api/storage/pools/{pool}/unlock`, `POST /api/storage/pools/{pool}/key` and `DELETE /api/storage/pools/{pool}/key/vault`; `pool.create` takes `encryptionKey` and `keepKeyInVault`; a pool has `encryption` (`locked`, `keyInVault`); new error codes `storage.encryption.key_invalid`, `storage.encryption.key_wrong`, `storage.encryption.key_required`, `storage.pool.not_encrypted`, `storage.pool.locked` and `storage.pool.not_locked`; `storage.encryption.unavailable` is no longer returned. For emergencies: the key sheet also carries the key in hex with the commands that open the pool on any Linux with OpenZFS, **Show key…** shows the vault's key again after the password and the authenticator code (`POST /api/storage/pools/{pool}/key/reveal`), a new key is confirmed by typing two of its groups, administrators get a storage alert while a pool is locked (`storage.alert.poolLocked`, `storage.alert.poolLockedVault`), and the new page "Encrypted pools: when something goes wrong" walks through every case. Further error codes `storage.encryption.vault_failed` and `storage.encryption.not_in_vault`.
- Pool keys on USB sticks (D385, #297): **Write key to USB stick…** on the card of an encrypted pool writes its key to a stick with a FAT, exFAT or ext file system (as `pithyx-keys/<pool GUID>.key`, never formatting the stick), optionally taking it out of the key vault. Whenever the stick is plugged in, at start or later, the box unlocks the pool; the stick can then be pulled, and the pool stays unlocked until the next restart, which the card and a notification say. A stick with an older key leaves the pool locked with a critical storage alert. Writing the vault's key needs the password and the authenticator code again; every key is checked against the pool before it is written. A typed key now also goes into the key vault on an unlocked pool once it is checked. New endpoints `GET /api/storage/usb-sticks` and `POST /api/storage/pools/{pool}/key/usb`; a pool's `encryption` has `usbKey`, `unlockedFromUsb` and `usbFailed`; new error codes `storage.usb.not_found`, `storage.usb.read_only`, `storage.usb.unreadable` and `storage.usb.write_failed`.
- Shortcuts to files and folders on the desktop (D382, #285): **Add to desktop** in the menu of Files, or drag entries from Files onto the desktop. A shortcut shows the file type's or the folder's icon with the shortcut arrow, opens like a double click in Files, and its menu has **Open with**, **Show in Files**, **Rename**, **Use original name** and **Remove**. It follows renames and moves made in Files, apps, WebDAV and FTP; one whose target is gone or no longer readable shows as missing. The desktop items API has the kind `file` with `share`, `path` and `target`.
- Passkeys and trusting a device (D380, #191): on a device that trusts the box, users sign in to the desktop with a passkey instead of the password and second factor (Pithyx menu > **Passkeys**, the button **Sign in with a passkey** and the browser's autofill on the sign-in page); a passkey belongs to the name the box was opened under. A device that does not trust the box gets the hint **Trust this device** after sign-in (also in the account menu and as an optional last step of the web setup) with the root certificate and the steps per system, and Settings > Certificates says whether the current device trusts the box. Once it does, a service worker keeps the desktop's files in the browser. Migration 0036; new error codes `auth.passkey.*`; the daemon uses Fido2 (fido2-net-lib, MIT) without its metadata service.
- Notifications by email and webhook (D379, #226): administrators set the SMTP server and the box's channels in Settings > Notifications, which get the system's alerts once; every user adds own channels under Notification channels in the user menu. Channels pick their events and a minimum level, send in the user's language, repeat nothing within 15 minutes and at most 20 an hour, and have a test button; webhooks send JSON (also for Gotify) or the ntfy format. Apps need the new permission `notifications.external`. Administrators hear about repeated failed sign-ins (`auth.signin.blocked`). The box renders these texts itself in English or German (#209). Migration 0035; new error codes `notifications.channel.*`, `notifications.smtp.*` and `notifications.language.unsupported`.
- Settings > Reverse proxy: proxy hosts and redirect hosts can take their certificate from Let's Encrypt or another ACME authority, for all their public domains over HTTP-01 on port 80, renewed by themselves, with the state and "Renew now" on each host and a notification when a renewal fails (D378, #194). Migration 0034; new error codes `proxy.acme.*`; `tools/dev-vm/acme-pebble.sh` runs the flow against Pebble.
- Settings > UPS: the box watches a UPS on USB (through its own NUT server) or of another NUT server, notifies the administrators on battery and shuts down cleanly at a charge or runtime limit, when the UPS reports a low battery or when the NUT server's primary forces it; a UPS on USB then cuts its power so the box starts again with the power (D377, #195). New error codes `system.ups.invalid` and `system.ups.unavailable`; the image installs `nut-server` and `nut-client`.
- Apps can add entries to the context menu of Files with the manifest's `fileActions`, for example "Extract here" on archives; the app opens with `launch.action` (the share, the folder and the selected names) and works on them with `files.shares` and the user's rights (D375). New error code `app.manifest.permission_missing`.

- `pithyx validate`, `lock`, `build`, `pack` and `dev` take `--allow-reserved-id`, and the Vite plugin `allowReservedId`, so the official catalog can check and pack its `org.pithyx.*` apps; a box still refuses such an id in a package from a file (D381, #269). The npm packages are at `1.1.0`.

- `pithyx-release sign-remote` (`tools/release/sign-remote.sh`): one command on the machine with the release key fetches an update file from the build machine over SSH, checks it, signs it, writes `channel.json` with the next sequence, copies the result back and with `--publish` uploads it to the release and the channel (D376).

- Files: **New file** in the menu of an empty spot creates an empty file and starts renaming it (#286). It has no button above the list, which would leave too little room for the path on a phone.
- `image/baseline/pithyx-1.1.0-amd64.packages`: the package list of the 1.1.0 image, the base for update files from 1.1.0 on.

### Changed

- The recycle bin in Files is a sortable table (name, original location, size, deleted, deleted by, kept until; a narrow window keeps the name, size and date) with **Restore** as the primary action; **Delete for good** and **Empty recycle bin** are no longer filled red buttons and still ask first (#328).
- Settings > **Home folders** shows the home pool and the default quota in form rows with the label on the left (#328).
- The account menu opens beside the account button in the Pithyx menu's left bar instead of above the menu (#328).
- Tables and virtualized lists are a chunk of their own that only the apps showing one load, so the desktop loaded after sign-in stays below its budget as the design pass brings tables to more apps (D400, #324).
- The Store keeps showing catalog apps this box cannot install (D393, #318): the badge **Not compatible**, and on the app's page why (a newer Pithyx needed, another processor, an older or newer app format) without an install button. The list of versions gives each version's reason. Before, such apps were left out.
- Personal settings (D392, #316): the account menu was twelve entries long and now has only **Personal settings** and **Appearance**. **Personal settings** is a window for every user, also in the Pithyx menu, with account and security (two-factor sign-in, passkeys, trust this device), the desktop (language, appearance, accent color, wallpaper, taskbar position, get desktop from), apps (default apps, open at sign-in) and notifications (channels); each row shows its current value and whether it applies to this device or to every device.
- Manifest version 2 (D388, #308), a breaking change of `pithyx.json`: `permissions` is a map from each permission or special right to its entry with a required, localized `reason`, shown in the install dialog and the Store; it replaces the list `permissions`, `backend.elevated` and `backend.optionalDevices` (a device entry takes `optional: true`, `network.internet` takes `hosts`). `arch` (`amd64`, `arm64`) and `source` (the repository URL or `private`) are required; a box of another architecture refuses the install (`app.arch.unsupported`). A special right without a backend is `app.elevated.backend_missing`. The box refuses version 1 manifests; the apps already installed keep working, because migration 0038 converts their stored manifests. `@pithyx/sdk`, `@pithyx/ui` and `@pithyx/cli` are at `2.0.0`, with the schema at `@pithyx/sdk/schema/manifest.v2.schema.json`.
- The favicon of the desktop and of pithyx.org is transparent and fills its square, with lighter faces on a dark tab bar, instead of a light tile.

### Removed

- Storage: **Take offline** and **Bring online** on the disks of a pool are gone, with the plan actions `pool.offline` and `pool.online` (now `storage.plan.action_invalid`). **Replace…** swaps a disk without taking it offline, and a disk that comes back after it was pulled is brought online by the box itself (D386, #304).

## [1.1.0] - 2026-10-24

Codename Carina (D374). Files opens files in the chosen app and takes drops on the path and the sidebar, apps can open after sign-in, snapshots show up live, and the SDK, UI kit and CLI leave the release candidates as 1.0.0. Like 1.0.0 it is marked beta on the stable channel: we consider it stable, but treat it as beta and keep backups. A box on 1.0.0 updates to it with one file; the path was tested from the 1.0.0 image (#187).

### Added

## [1.0.0] - 2026-10-18

The first release, codename Pyxis (D345, D366): the NAS core, the file services, the app runtime with the Store, the installer and the updater. It is marked beta: we consider it stable, but it has not yet proven itself on real boxes in everyday use, so treat it as beta and keep backups. Fixes come as 1.0.x on the stable channel.

### Known limitations

- The box's certificate authority keeps no revocation list, and Let's Encrypt is not built in; the setup step "Trust this device", passkeys and the service worker come later.
- Pool encryption (ZFS native, key vault) is not built, so data that needs encryption does not belong on a box yet.
- Every elevated app right is refused with `app.elevated.unsupported`: an app that asks for `host.network`, `host.pid`, a device, a capability, `docker.socket` or `host.full` cannot be installed.

### Security

- SMB sessions end when their user's groups change (D353): smbd kept a session's groups from its start, so a new right did not apply and a revoked shared folder stayed usable over SMB until the user signed out of the computer. The daemon now ends the user's sessions after the change, and the client reconnects with the current rights.
